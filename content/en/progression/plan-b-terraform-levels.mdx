## Understanding the Dual Progression Architecture

Progression in *Plan B: Terraform* does not follow a simple, linear quest list. Instead, the game operates on two interconnected axes: **Civic/City Growth Levels** and **Planetary Terraforming Levels**. 

Civic progression relies on delivering specific manufactured goods to expanding colonies. As your urban centers receive food, fresh water, and construction materials, their population levels climb. In turn, hit population thresholds trigger structural upgrades that unlock advanced manufacturing recipes, heavy transport options, and higher-tier extraction machinery.

Planetary terraforming progression runs parallel to your factories. As you manufacture and vent greenhouse gases, warm the permafrost, manage sea-level equilibrium, and seed forests, global environmental meters tick upward. If you push industrial milestones without monitoring environmental **plan b terraform levels**, melting ice caps can easily flood supply depots and rail corridors.

| Progression Pillar | Core Metric | Primary Driver | Primary Unlocks / Rewards |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **City Milestones** | Total Planet Population | Consistent delivery of citizen goods | Trains, High-Speed Rail, Advanced Assembly Lines |
| **Atmospheric Warming** | Global Temperature (°C) | Greenhouse gas generator emissions | Permafrost melting, surface water formation |
| **Hydrosphere** | Global Water Volume & Sea Level | Controlled glacial melting and pumping | Rivers, oceans, atmospheric humidity |
| **Biosphere** | Biomass & Tree Cover | Soil enrichment and forest planting | Oxygen generation, ecosystem stabilization |

## City Expansion and Population Milestone Levels

Colony cities serve as both your resource sinks and your gateway to technological progression. Cities begin as small outpost domes and steadily transform into sprawling, multi-district megalopolises as you feed them higher-tier consumer goods.

Reaching designated population thresholds unlocks critical logistics equipment. If you neglect steady delivery schedules, citizens will not consume goods, population growth stalls, and your progression stops cold.

```
Outpost (Pop < 500) ➔ Metropolis (Pop 5,000) ➔ Regional Capital (Pop 20,000) ➔ Planetary Megacity (Pop 40,000+)
```

Community reports show that setting up buffer storage outside city boundaries prevents traffic gridlock from choking critical water and food deliveries. Below is the full breakdown of how city growth scales through the progression tiers.

| City Level / Tier | Population Requirement | Critical Demands | Unlocked Infrastructure & Recipes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tier 1: Outpost** | 0 – 500 | Water (Pumping Ice) | Basic Trucks, Truck Stops, Concrete, Mechanical Parts |
| **Tier 2: Township** | 500 – 2,500 | Concrete, Steel Beams | Rail Depots, Cargo Trains, High-Capacity Extractors |
| **Tier 3: City** | 2,500 – 10,000 | Food, Oxygen, Advanced Parts | High-Speed Trains, Greenhouse Gas Generators, Polymers |
| **Tier 4: Metropolis** | 10,000 – 25,000 | Consumer Electronics, Complex Alloys | Atmospheric Filtration, Mega Pumping Stations |
| **Tier 5: Megacity** | 25,000 – 40,000+ | Luxury Goods, Composite Systems | Planetary Launch Systems, End-Game Biome Controls |

### Strategies for Climbing City Levels Efficiently

1. **Prioritize Water Above All Else:** Water is the fundamental fuel of urban expansion. Even late-game cities with access to complex consumer products will stagnate immediately if their water buffer empties.
2. **Decouple Inbound Deliveries:** Never route all delivery trucks or trains into a single terminal. Separate your food, water, and structural material drops across different sectors of the city perimeter.
3. **Use Decentralized Outposts:** In the early **plan b terraform levels**, players often try to feed three or four cities simultaneously. Veteran player experience shows it is significantly faster to concentrate resources into a single primary city until you unlock trains.

## Terraforming Stages: From Frozen Desert to Living Biosphere

While your cities expand, your primary mission is to alter the planet's atmospheric and geological profile. The environmental **plan b terraform levels** represent hard ecological shifts that dynamically rewrite the planet's geography.

Understanding each environmental phase prevents disaster and prepares your factories for new harvesting opportunities, such as extracting liquid surface water instead of mining solid ice blocks.

| Terraforming Phase | Key Trigger / Target | Environmental Effects | Player Hazards & Opportunities |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Phase 1: Deep Freeze** | Base Starting State (< -40°C) | Dry surface, frozen permafrost, solid ice fields | Limited to ice mining; no surface water flows |
| **Phase 2: Thermal Thaw** | Global Temp reaches 0°C | Ice melts into flowing water; rivers form | Low-lying factories can flood; liquid water extraction unlocks |
| **Phase 3: Ocean Formation** | Sea Level reaches standard datum | Depressions fill into lakes and oceans | Major transit corridors may be submerged; shipping unlocks |
| **Phase 4: Green Transition** | Atmospheric Humidity & Soil prep | Organic moss, lichen, and small scrub grow | Agricultural fields become significantly more productive |
| **Phase 5: Native Forestation** | Oxygen > 15%, Stable Hydrosphere | Full tree growth, self-sustaining water cycle | Complete environmental stability; ultimate milestone reached |

### Phase 1 to Phase 2: Atmospheric Heating Mechanics

To push past the freezing threshold, you must manufacture and vent greenhouse gases (primarily fluorinated gases or sulfur hexafluoride). This requires discovering sulfur deposits and setting up dedicated chemical synthesis hubs. 

Early atmospheric warming is a slow burn. Monitor the planet's topography view before warming begins. Check natural elevation lines so your initial factories are not located in dry basins destined to become sea basins.

### Phase 3 to Phase 5: Hydrosphere and Biosphere Management

Once the temperature passes 0°C, natural liquid water emerges. This transforms your logistics: surface water pumps produce fluid exponentially faster than mechanical miners extract ice.

However, water accumulation must be carefully watched. If you melt the polar ice caps too aggressively without balancing atmospheric humidity, ocean levels will rise past safe thresholds, drowning nearby mining camps.

## Logistics Scaling Across Production Levels

You cannot beat the higher **plan b terraform levels** relying on the same logistics you built during the first hour of play. As supply chains multiply in complexity, simple point-to-point truck routes collapse under congestion.

Progression requires transitioning through three distinct logistical eras: Road, Rail, and High-Speed Maglev networks.

| Logistics Tier | Unlocked At | Best Use Case | Maximum Throughput | Key Bottleneck |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Basic Trucks** | Start of Game | Short-range depot transfers (< 50 tiles) | Low to Moderate | Road crossing congestion |
| **Cargo Trains** | City Tier 2 (Pop 500+) | Long-distance bulk raw resource transit | High | Rail network intersections & signaling |
| **High-Speed Rail** | City Tier 3 (Pop 2,500+) | Massive inter-continental supply arteries | Extreme | Power demand & track infrastructure cost |
| **Cargo Drones/Pipes** | Mid-to-Late Game | Dedicated fluid/gas routing | Constant Stream | Distance degradation & pipe throughput |

```
Mining Nodes ➔ Short-Haul Trucks ➔ Central Freight Depot ➔ Cargo Trains ➔ City Hub
```

Player experience demonstrates that the most common reason for stalled progress in the middle **plan b terraform levels** is an overreliance on trucks for cross-map raw resource hauling. Trucks should strictly serve as local feeder units that gather raw ores from miners and drop them off at dedicated rail loading stations.

## Industrial Production Chains by Progression Tier

Advancing through the game requires converting base minerals—Iron, Sulfur, Carbon, and Ice—into increasingly intricate components. Below is an overview of how your factory architecture should evolve across progression tiers.

| Tech Tier | Inputs Required | Manufactured End Products | Primary Strategic Purpose |

### Factory Layout Optimization

To prevent production shutdowns as you climb the intermediate **plan b terraform levels**, adopt a modular factory footprint. Build independent smelting quadrants with dedicated output buffers rather than weaving one giant, interconnected production floor. 

When a city unlocks a new demand, such as composite building panels, you can simply attach a dedicated manufacturing node to an existing rail line without rebuilding your core iron foundries.

## Advanced Strategies to Clear Late-Game Milestones

Reaching the final tiers of planet development tests every logistical system you have assembled. Here are proven strategies for conquering late-stage hurdles:

- **Survey Elevation Maps Early:** Switch on topographic overlays before building high-capacity train junctions. Keep all major rail arteries above the projected sea level line to prevent permanent disruptions when oceans fill.
- **Isolate Food and Water Transport:** Never put non-essential industrial goods on the same freight line that supplies municipal water or food. If a steel warehouse backs up, it could halt a multi-freight train carrying life-critical water, causing rapid population collapse.
- **Pre-Produce Infrastructure Hardware:** Transitioning from simple trains to high-speed rail networks requires immense amounts of concrete and specialized steel. Build a dedicated stockpile of tracks and stations hours before you plan to lay down high-speed continental rail.
- **Maintain Atmospheric Equilibrium:** Once global temperatures hit target ranges for liquid water, ramp down greenhouse gas production. Over-warming will melt all remaining ice caps, completely flooding low-elevation continents and destroying vital resource nodes.

Smoothly transitioning through all **plan b terraform levels** comes down to anticipating the demands of the next population tier before your current city hits its growth cap.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### How many Plan B Terraform levels exist for city milestones?
There are five core civic milestone tiers in *Plan B: Terraform*. Progression begins with initial Outpost settlements (under 500 citizens) and expands progressively through Township, City, Metropolis, and ultimately Megacity status (40,000+ citizens). Each tier unlocks new transport infrastructure, manufacturing recipes, and extraction technologies.

### Can rising sea levels permanently destroy my production hubs?
Yes. When global temperatures rise past 0°C, melting permafrost and glacial fields create dynamic water flows that fill low-lying basins. Any depots, extractors, roads, or rail lines built below the natural sea level datum will submerge and stop functioning. Always check terrain elevation overlays before building permanent heavy industry.

### What is the fastest way to progress through the early Plan B Terraform levels?
The fastest strategy is to focus all early industrial production on single-product manufacturing lines that feed a single designated city. Avoid spreading your basic truck capacity across multiple settlements. Concentrate on pumping ice, producing concrete, and supplying your main outpost to unlock cargo trains as quickly as possible.

### When should I replace my truck fleets with cargo trains?
You should begin transitioning to cargo trains the moment you reach City Tier 2 (500 population). Trucks work well for short-distance gathering within 50 tiles of a mining node, but long-distance resource transfers will quickly cause traffic bottlenecks that choke growth during later **plan b terraform levels**. Use trucks as local feeders and trains as your long-haul backbone.
